## 1. 4C Learning Concept

**What is the 4C learning model and how does it structure effective training sessions?**

- Four phases: **Connect, Concept, Concrete Practice, Conclusion**
- **Connect**: activate prior knowledge and link to learner's experience
- **Concept**: present new content in digestible, structured chunks
- **Concrete Practice**: hands-on exercises to apply and internalize knowledge
- **Conclusion**: summarize, reflect, and plan transfer to real work
- Architect uses 4C to **teach architecture decisions** to dev teams
- Active learning > passive lectures; **70-20-10** model supports this

## 2. Communication

**Why is communication the architect's most critical skill, and what models guide effective communication?**

- **Schulz von Thun**: every message has 4 sides (fact, self-reveal, relationship, appeal)
- **Active listening**: paraphrase, ask open questions, withhold judgment
- **Nonviolent Communication** (Rosenberg): observation, feeling, need, request
- Architects spend **~70% of time communicating**, not coding
- **Adapt communication style** to audience (C-level vs developers vs testers)
- Miscommunication is the **#1 cause** of architectural misalignment

## 3. Peer Feedback (PEW Method)

**How does the PEW method structure constructive peer feedback?**

- **PEW**: Perception, Effect, Wish — structured feedback framework
- **Perception**: describe observable behavior without interpretation
- **Effect**: explain the impact of the behavior on you or the team
- **Wish**: state a concrete, actionable change request
- Feedback should be **timely, specific, and behavior-focused** (not personal)
- Receiving: **listen, ask clarifying questions**, don't defend immediately

## 4. Leadership Without Formal Power

**How can architects lead effectively without formal authority over teams?**

- Architects often have **influence but no direct authority** over dev teams
- **Expert power**: credibility through technical competence and hands-on work
- **Referent power**: trust built through relationships and consistent behavior
- **Servant leadership**: remove obstacles, enable teams, share credit
- Use **persuasion over prescription**: explain WHY behind architecture decisions
- **Build coalitions**: align with tech leads, POs, and key developers first

## 5. Motivation

**What motivational theories help architects understand and influence team behavior?**

- **Intrinsic motivation** (autonomy, mastery, purpose) outperforms extrinsic rewards
- **Pink's Drive**: Autonomy, Mastery, Purpose as three pillars of motivation
- **Herzberg**: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction
- **Self-Determination Theory**: competence, autonomy, relatedness are universal needs
- Architects foster motivation by **giving teams ownership** of design decisions
- Avoid **micromanagement** — it destroys autonomy and intrinsic motivation

## 6. Stakeholder Presentation

**How should architects present architecture decisions to different stakeholder groups?**

- **Know your audience**: C-level wants business impact; devs want technical detail
- **Pyramid Principle** (Minto): lead with conclusion, then supporting arguments
- Use **visual aids**: C4 diagrams, architecture decision matrices, trade-off tables
- **Storytelling**: frame decisions as solutions to stakeholder problems
- Address **risks and trade-offs** proactively — don't wait to be asked
- Keep presentations **short and focused**; detailed backup in appendix

## 7. Change Management and Fearless Change

**How does the Fearless Change pattern language help architects drive organizational adoption?**

- **Fearless Change** (Rising/Manns): 48 patterns for introducing new ideas
- **Evangelist**: passionate advocate who persistently promotes the change
- **Small Successes**: demonstrate quick wins to build momentum and credibility
- **Ask for Help**: involve skeptics early to convert resistance into ownership
- **Dedicated Champion**: senior ally who provides political cover and resources
- Change is **emotional, not just rational**; address feelings alongside facts

---

## 8. Coaching and Sparring

**How do coaching and sparring sessions support architect development and team growth?**

- **Coaching**: guide through questions, don't give answers directly
- **Sparring**: peer-level challenge of ideas to stress-test architecture decisions
- **GROW model**: Goal, Reality, Options, Will — structured coaching conversations
- Architects need **both coaching (receive) and mentoring (give)** roles
- **Socratic questioning** helps teams discover solutions independently
- Regular sparring prevents **confirmation bias** in architecture decisions

---

## 9. Self-Leadership and the Responsibility Process

**How does the Responsibility Process help architects move from blame to ownership?**

- **Responsibility Process** (Avery): Denial → Blame → Justify → Shame → Obligation → Responsibility
- Only at **Responsibility** level do you have full power to act and change
- **Quit** is an escape at any stage — choosing to disengage from the problem
- Self-leadership requires **awareness** of which stage you're currently in
- Architects model **ownership culture** by taking responsibility for failures
- **Intention + Awareness + Confront** are the three keys to reaching Responsibility
